Precedent Study: Sketchpad

Datasheet

Title: Sketchpad: A Man-Machine Graphical Communication System

Author: Ivan Edward Sutherland

Year: 1963

Format: Interactive graphical computer program; PhD thesis and technical report; early CAD / HCI prototype.

Intended Audience: Computer science and engineering researchers at MIT and affiliated labs; military and industrial sponsors; early HCI and computer graphics communities.

Project Summary

Sketchpad is an interactive graphical computer program developed by Ivan Sutherland at MIT’s Lincoln Laboratory in 1963 as part of his PhD thesis. Running on the TX-2 computer, Sketchpad allowed a user to draw, edit, and manipulate geometric shapes directly on a CRT screen using a light pen. It is widely regarded as the ancestor of modern computer-aided design (CAD) systems and one of the first complete graphical user interfaces.

Technically, Sketchpad introduced several key concepts: hierarchical master–instance structures (a prototype–instance relationship later echoed in object-oriented programming), an early form of entity–component data modeling, and geometric constraints that allowed users to specify relationships such as fixed lengths, perpendicular lines, and coincident points. The system then enforced these constraints by recalculating the drawing.

Culturally and historically, Sketchpad demonstrated that computers could be used not only for numerical computation but also for visual thinking and design. It reframed the computer as an interactive partner in drawing and reasoning about geometry, profoundly influencing later work in HCI, GUI design, CAD, and interactive graphics.
